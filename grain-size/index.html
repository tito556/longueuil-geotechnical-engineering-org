Grain Size Analysis (Sieve + Hydrometer) in Longueuil: Reliable Soil Gradation Testing

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

The geotechnical contrast between Vieux-Longueuil's compact glacial till and the deep Champlain Sea clay deposits near the Saint Lawrence River in LeMoyne dictates every foundation decision we make. What works on the terrace gravels won't necessarily perform three kilometers north where the marine silt can extend past twenty meters. A thorough grain size analysis—combining mechanical sieving for the coarse fraction and hydrometer sedimentation for fines—is the only way to quantify that variability. We run the full ASTM D422 procedure because partial gradation curves, the kind you get from a quick field shake test, won't tell you enough about drainage potential or frost susceptibility. In our experience across the South Shore, the split between sand and fines often shifts within a single borehole, and knowing exactly where that boundary sits changes the footings design parameters and the required compaction specs.

A complete sieve-plus-hydrometer curve reveals far more than sand-silt-clay percentages—it governs permeability estimates, frost-heave potential, and the selection of filter materials for subsurface drainage.

How we work

Longueuil's urban expansion after the 1960s, when farmland and orchard tracts were rapidly converted into residential subdivisions, left a patchwork of engineered fill over the original post-glacial stratigraphy. We routinely encounter layers where imported granular material sits directly on sensitive silty clay, and without a complete grain size distribution curve, that interface is easy to misinterpret. Our laboratory procedure starts with a representative split of the sample, dried and weighed, then passed through a stack of sieves from 75 mm down to 75 microns. The minus-200 fraction goes into a sedimentation cylinder with sodium hexametaphosphate dispersant, and we take hydrometer readings at precise time intervals per ASTM D7928. The resulting combined curve gives us D10, D30, and D60 values that feed directly into the coefficient of uniformity and coefficient of curvature—numbers that a triaxial shear strength correlation or a Proctor moisture-density relationship can't be properly interpreted without. For Longueuil's typical silty fine sands, we often see Cu values between 2 and 8, indicating a poorly to uniformly graded material that demands careful drainage planning.

Local geotechnical context

A six-story residential project on Chemin de Chambly ran into unexpected settlement issues because the pre-construction investigation relied on a sieve-only analysis that treated everything passing the 200-sieve as a single 'fines' category. The hydrometer portion we ran afterward revealed that nearly forty percent of that fines fraction was actually sensitive clay with a liquidity index above 1.2—a completely different consolidation behavior than the silty fill assumed in the original report. That oversight delayed the project by four months and required a redesign of the deep excavations support system. In Longueuil's Champlain Sea deposits, skipping the sedimentation analysis on a sample that looks 'mostly granular' in the field is a gamble that can trigger differential settlement, basement water ingress, and disputes with the municipality over foundation drain performance. The NBCC 2015 and CSA A23.3 both expect a defensible gradation curve when classifying site soils for bearing capacity and seismic site class determination.

Questions and answers

What is the typical cost for a grain size analysis with hydrometer in Longueuil?

When opting for a standard sieve-plus-hydrometer package under ASTM D422, costs vary from CA$140 to CA$280 per sample. This range depends on whether it is a single point or part of a batch testing for a larger study. The hydrometer-only service falls at the lower portion of that price range.

How long does it take to get results from a sieve and hydrometer test?

A minimum of 24 hours is needed for the hydrometer sedimentation readings alone. After including oven-drying, sieving, and data reduction, a final gradation report is usually delivered within three to five business days. If the project timeline requires it, expedited processing can reduce that to two days.

Why is the hydrometer test necessary if the soil looks sandy?

Sands that seem clean in the field frequently contain five to fifteen percent fines, and this minor fraction governs drainage and frost susceptibility. For Longueuil's post-glacial soils, the hydrometer test determines the proportion of silt versus actual clay in that passing material, influencing permeability assessments and subgrade drain design.

Do you provide the grain size distribution in digital format for modeling software?

Absolutely, we provide the full gradation table and semi-log plot in CSV, PDF, or formats that work with standard geotechnical software. The D10, D30, and D60 values are consistently tabulated, ready for immediate application in filter design and seepage analysis.
