Underground Excavations in Longueuil

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Urban densification, the need to protect surface infrastructure, and Longueuil's harsh winters drive demand for subterranean spaces like tunnels, basements, parking garages, and utility corridors. These underground excavations involve preliminary site assessments, geotechnical design, construction, and ongoing monitoring. Given the city's location within the Greater Montreal area, specialized geotechnical expertise is essential to ensure structural stability, groundwater control, and protection of adjacent buildings.

The geology of Longueuil features Quaternary marine clays from the Champlain Sea overlaying glacial till and competent sedimentary bedrock of the Trenton and Utica groups. These sensitive, compressible clays can cause significant settlement when disturbed. Depth to bedrock varies, influencing excavation methods such as open-cut, shoring, controlled blasting, or mechanical rock removal. A high water table in permeable till and fractured rock layers necessitates robust dewatering and waterproofing to prevent instability and flooding.

Excavations in Longueuil must comply with the Quebec Construction Code (incorporating the National Building Code) for structural design, the Safety Code for the Construction Industry (CSTC) for trench safety and protective systems, and the Explosives Act for rock blasting. Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec (OIQ) guidelines limit vibration and overpressure, while the Environment Quality Act governs groundwater discharge and contaminated soil disposal.

The types of projects requiring underground excavations in Longueuil are diverse. They include deep foundations and multi-level basements for residential and commercial towers in developing hubs, the installation and repair of municipal water and sewer mains, and the construction of underground transit infrastructure. Specialized work, such as microtunneling for utility crossings beneath major roadways or rivers, is increasingly common to minimize surface disruption. For any such project, a critical component of risk management is the implementation of geotechnical excavation monitoring, which uses instruments like inclinometers, piezometers, and vibration monitors to track ground movement and provide early warning of potential instability, protecting both workers and adjacent property. The success of these complex undertakings hinges on a thorough understanding of local soil behavior and the application of advanced engineering controls from the planning phase through to construction completion.

Questions and answers

What are the main geotechnical risks associated with underground excavations in Longueuil?

Major hazards include ground settlement and instability from sensitive marine clays, flooding or base heave from a high water table, and vibration-induced damage to nearby structures during rock excavation. Preliminary geotechnical surveys help mitigate these risks before construction.

What permits are required for an underground excavation project in Longueuil?

A professional engineer must seal all shoring and structural designs per the Quebec Professional Code. Municipal excavation permits from Longueuil are required, and deeper projects may need provincial approvals for groundwater management under the Environment Quality Act and for explosive use.

How is groundwater managed during deep excavations in this region?

Permanent below-grade structures incorporate waterproofing membranes and drainage systems to resist hydrostatic pressure. During excavation, dewatering systems like deep wells or wellpoints lower the water table, with careful rate control to avoid triggering settlement in sensitive clays.

Why is vibration monitoring important during rock excavation in urban areas?

Real-time vibration and overpressure monitoring, following OIQ guidelines, ensures that rock excavation methods like blasting or hydraulic hammering stay within safe limits to protect older masonry buildings in Longueuil from damage, thereby reducing liability.
