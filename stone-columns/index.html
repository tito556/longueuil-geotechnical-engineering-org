Stone Column Design in Longueuil – Ground Improvement for Soft Soils

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Longueuil sits on the south shore of the St. Lawrence River, where the subsurface is dominated by deep, compressible Champlain Sea clays deposited over 10,000 years ago. These sensitive silty clays can lose strength when disturbed, making conventional shallow foundations risky for anything beyond light residential structures. A stone column design bridges the gap between poor native soil and the structural loads of mid-rise buildings, tanks, and embankments. On sites between Vieux-Longueuil and Saint-Hubert, we often see 8 to 25 meters of soft clay before reaching competent till, and that demands a ground improvement strategy that controls both settlement and bearing capacity. The vibrocompaction process can densify granular fills, but where the fine-grained matrix dominates, stone columns installed by wet or dry bottom-feed methods become the reliable alternative.

A well-designed stone column grid in Champlain Sea clay can reduce post-construction settlement by over 70 percent compared to untreated ground, while providing a verified drainage path for seismic pore pressure relief.

How we work

The most common mistake we see on Longueuil job sites is ordering a stone column quantity based solely on a target area replacement ratio without verifying column length against the depth of the compressible layer. A design that stops columns short of the competent stratum leaves a compressible bulb underneath, and differential settlement shows up within the first two freeze-thaw cycles. Our approach ties the column grid, diameter, and length directly to CPTu pore pressure dissipation data and lab consolidation curves from undisturbed Shelby tube samples. For silos and heavily loaded slabs, we often pair the stone column grid with a mat foundation to distribute stress uniformly and reduce the number of columns required. On sites near the river where liquefiable silts appear within the upper 6 meters, the column array also functions as a drainage path, and we cross-check the design using liquefaction triggering analyses per NCEER methodology to ensure excess pore pressure dissipates during a seismic event.

Local geotechnical context

A crawler crane outfitted with a 130–180 kW vibrator is employed for dry bottom-feed vibroflot installation in Longueuil. Stone is fed through a hopper and down a central tube to the tip. Within the sensitive clays of the Montérégie area, careful control of withdrawal rate and amperage is required: too rapid lifting leads to necking, while excessive dwell remolds the clay, possibly reducing its shear strength by 40% or more. During column construction, real-time data acquisition records depth, amperage, and stone consumption every 0.5 m. Post-installation verification uses a full-depth probe or, on critical projects, a short CPT sounding through the column center. A neck missed at 4 m depth in a Longueuil industrial slab may result in 50 mm differential settlement, causing cracks in partition walls and binding of overhead doors.

Complementary services

01

Feasibility and design

To establish column diameter, grid spacing, and depth, we analyze CPTu, consolidation tests, and shear vane results. PLAXIS or FLAC numerical models then estimate settlement reduction and bearing capacity improvement for both static and seismic conditions.

02

Load test and verification

Design assumptions are validated through full-scale modulus tests and zone load tests on individual columns and groups. Settlement plates and piezometers are installed in test zones to enable long-term monitoring.

03

Construction oversight and QA/QC

Field engineers record per-column installation parameters including depth, amperage, stone volume, and lift thickness. Daily reports and as-built documentation are prepared for the project files and municipal permit closure.

Questions and answers

How much does a stone column design for a Longueuil project cost?

In Longueuil, professional fees for stone column design and field verification usually fall between CA$2,030 and CA$8,070, varying with treated area, column count, and instrumentation needs. A commercial pad smaller than 500 m² is at the low end, while a multi-building industrial project with zone load tests and piezometer arrays approaches the high end.

Why use stone columns instead of rigid piles in Champlain Sea clay?

Rather than bypassing the soil, stone columns enhance the whole soil mass. For Longueuil clays, rigid piles carry loads to deep till, an effective but more expensive solution requiring structural connections. Stone columns directly mitigate settlement, provide drainage for seismic pore pressure, and enable lighter, more economical ground-floor slabs.

How do you confirm the columns were installed correctly?

Each column is verified in real time during installation via data acquisition of depth, vibrator amperage, and stone consumption per lift. Following installation, selected columns undergo full-depth probing and possible CPT sounding. For critical structures, a zone load test with settlement plates yields direct modulus and deformation measurements.

Can stone columns be installed year-round in Longueuil?

It is feasible with careful planning. The dry bottom-feed method can be used in frozen conditions provided the working platform is kept in good condition. Prior to column installation, we usually lay a 1.0–1.5 m granular working mat that protects the rig from frost-susceptible surface soils and offers a stable base for the crawler crane.
