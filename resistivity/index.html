Electrical Resistivity Surveys (VES) in Longueuil

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

The transformation of Longueuil from a seigneurial-era farming parish into a bustling South Shore center placed significant infrastructure atop the deep Champlain Sea clay layer that underlies the area. Engineers familiar with the Greenfield Park and Vieux-Longueuil benches recognize that boreholes alone often fail to capture the sharp lateral changes in clay thickness or the variable bedrock surface that affect settlement and excavation stability. Vertical Electrical Soundings (VES), a resistivity technique, deliver a continuous profile of subsurface resistivity variations without requiring extensive drilling. By sending a known current through electrode arrays and measuring the resulting voltage difference, we delineate the shift from silty top deposits to the conductive marine clays and then to the resistive till and underlying sedimentary rock layers. This nondestructive method, performed according to ASTM D6431-18, enables Longueuil engineers to identify paleochannels, locate cleaner sand pockets, and evaluate groundwater salinity—factors essential for designing deep foundations near the St. Lawrence River or planning dewatering for excavations along Taschereau Boulevard, where soil conditions can change drastically within a single block.

Low-resistivity Champlain clay versus high-resistivity till and bedrock creates a stark electrical contrast that makes VES exceptionally effective on the South Shore.

How we work

In Longueuil, we frequently encounter situations where contractors have drilled a few boreholes along a proposed building footprint and still face uncertainty about the continuity of a buried sand lens or a soft clay seam between the test locations. VES fills that gap by measuring apparent resistivity at progressively deeper investigation depths, generating a 1D layered model that can be correlated with SPT drilling logs to calibrate the geophysical response. A saturated Champlain clay, with its low resistivity of roughly 5 to 30 ohm-m, stands out sharply against a glacial till or a fractured shale bedrock that might read above 100 ohm-m, making VES an efficient tool for mapping the depth to competent bearing strata across an entire site. When the resistivity profile suggests a potential granular layer within the clay sequence, we often recommend targeted CPT testing to confirm the mechanical properties and drainage characteristics of that layer, which can significantly alter the foundation design assumptions. Our field crews use Schlumberger and Wenner array configurations depending on the target depth and site access constraints, processing the data with iterative inversion software to produce a geoelectrical cross-section that is directly usable by the structural and geotechnical engineering teams.

Local geotechnical context

The VES survey gear deployed in Longueuil includes a resistivity meter equipped with a high-impedance voltmeter, stainless steel electrodes, and insulated cable reels that can span hundreds of meters across the site. A specific difficulty in the South Shore is the prevalence of buried metal utilities and reinforced concrete foundations in former industrial zones, which generate stray currents and can short-circuit the electric field if the electrode array is placed too near. Our team always conducts a utility locate and, if needed, switches to offset arrays or reduces electrode spacing to avoid interference with underground metal. The high conductivity of Champlain Sea clay poses another challenge: substantial signal attenuation with depth restricts the maximum investigation depth unless the injected current is increased. We address this by employing higher-power transmitters and meticulously lowering contact resistance at each electrode, often applying a saline solution to the ground in very dry summer conditions to maintain reliable current injection and clean data collection throughout the sounding sequence.

Complementary services

01

1D Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES)

Single-location resistivity soundings determine layer thicknesses and resistivity values beneath a point. They are perfect for matching with existing borehole logs and extending the soil profile across a site.

02

2D Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT)

Multi-electrode profiling along a line produces a continuous cross-section of subsurface resistivity distribution. It is used to map lateral variations in clay thickness, detect paleochannels, and find the bedrock surface beneath variable overburden.

Relevant standards

The relevant standards include ASTM D6431-18 (Standard Guide for Using the Direct Current Resistivity Method), NBCC 2020 (National Building Code of Canada – geotechnical investigation requirements), and CAN/BNQ 2501-250 (Geotechnical site investigation standards for the St. Lawrence Lowlands).

Questions and answers

How does VES complement a standard geotechnical investigation in Longueuil?

Boreholes offer precise data at specific points, but Champlain Sea clay can change considerably in thickness and composition between them. VES delivers continuous resistivity information that fills these gaps, aiding in identifying transitions among clay, till, and bedrock, and highlighting potential anomalies such as buried sand channels that a standard drilling pattern might overlook.

What is the typical depth of investigation for a VES survey on the South Shore?

Using Schlumberger arrays with electrode spreads up to 200 m, we typically achieve investigation depths of 40 to 50 m—enough to go through the entire Champlain Sea sequence and detect the top of glacial till or bedrock in most parts of Longueuil. The actual depth is constrained by the maximum current output and the conductivity of the near-surface clay.

What is the approximate cost of an electrical resistivity survey in Longueuil?

A VES program in Longueuil generally costs between CA$870 and CA$1,260, with the final price depending on the number of soundings, required array length, site access, and the complexity of data processing and inversion needed to generate the final geoelectrical model.

Can VES be performed in winter when the ground is frozen in Longueuil?

Yes, but frozen ground forms a high-resistivity surface layer that hinders current injection. We use longer electrodes to penetrate below the frost line and, when necessary, apply a saline solution at the contact points to reduce resistance and ensure sufficient current reaches the unfrozen soil beneath.
