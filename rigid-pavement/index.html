Rigid Pavement Design in Longueuil: Concrete Performance for Quebec Winters

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Given that a water table dramatically rises and falls with the seasons in Longueuil—a city with over 250,000 residents on terrain just 20 meters above the St. Lawrence River—and that much of the South Shore rests on sensitive Champlain Sea clay, rigid pavement design becomes a structural issue as much as a civil one. In this environment, a poorly designed pavement would be destroyed within two freeze-thaw cycles by the moisture conditions acting on concrete slabs. For any Longueuil assignment, our engineers lead with drainage considerations: a saturated subgrade will cause even a 300 mm slab on granular base to pump fines and lose its support. The AASHTO 93 rigid pavement method is our standard, where we reconcile fatigue consumption and erosion damage with the actual traffic mix supplied by the design engineer, and we always complete the frost-depth verification required by the Quebec provincial supplement to the TAC pavement guide.

A rigid pavement in Longueuil fails first at the joints, not in the slab center: water, frost, and loss of base support drive 90 percent of the distress we see.

How we work

The temperature swing in Longueuil is one of the most aggressive in southern Quebec. A concrete pavement placed in July at 35 °C will contract more than 4 mm per slab length when January hits minus 30 °C, and that movement has to be absorbed by joint design and load-transfer steel. We specify dowel diameter and spacing using the PCA and FHWA joint detailing recommendations, correlating slab thickness with the k-value obtained from plate load tests on the prepared subgrade. For industrial yards where forklift axle loads exceed 10 tonnes, we often shift from a standard 200 mm slab to a structural section with 230 mm or more, reinforced with mesh in the top third to control crack width. A proper rigid pavement in this region also depends on the quality of the open-graded drainage layer: without it, the hydraulic gradient under the slab edge will erode the subbase in two winters. The CBR road subgrade evaluation protocol gives us the stiffness ratio we need to verify that the granular support will not degrade under repetitive truck loading.

Local geotechnical context

Our repeated findings on the South Shore indicate that the primary risk to rigid pavement comes from differential heave due to frost-susceptible silts trapped in the subgrade, not from the concrete mix. When a slab corner elevates 15 mm above the adjacent panel, dowel bars seize and joint spalling occurs by the first spring. Numerous zones in Longueuil have natural silty clay with a frost susceptibility index above 20 mm²/h; these necessitate full-depth granular replacement or a rigid insulation layer under the slab. A common local issue is underestimating industrial traffic: a distribution center in the Saint-Hubert sector can have 80 fully loaded B-trains per day, causing fatigue consumption that exhausts the slab's design life in 12 years instead of the assumed 25. We insist on a precise ESAL count backed by weigh-in-motion data or conservative axle-load assumptions prior to finalizing thickness design.

Questions and answers

What thickness of rigid pavement is needed for a Longueuil municipal road?

For a standard two-lane municipal road with bus traffic and occasional heavy trucks, the AASHTO 93 rigid pavement method typically yields a plain jointed concrete slab of 200 to 230 mm, assuming a 150 mm granular subbase over a properly compacted clay subgrade. The 20-year ESAL projection governs final thickness: 200 mm for 1 million ESALs and 230 mm or more for 5 million ESALs. All concrete must have 5–7% air entrainment and a minimum 28-day compressive strength of 32 MPa per CSA A23.1.

How much does a rigid pavement design cost in Longueuil?

A full rigid pavement design package—including subgrade investigation, plate load testing, AASHTO 93 thickness analysis, joint detailing, and a stamped engineering report—costs between CA$2,240 and CA$8,690. Price depends on pavement area, number of soil investigation points, and traffic data complexity. A small commercial parking lot with one borehole and standard traffic assumptions falls at the low end, while a multi-street industrial subdivision with full geotechnical profiling and ESAL modeling reaches the high end.

How do Longueuil's clay soils affect rigid pavement performance?

Underlying much of Longueuil, the Champlain Sea clay is highly frost-susceptible and loses bearing capacity when saturated. Without drainage and frost protection, it heaves unevenly in winter, lifting slab corners and breaking joint load transfer. Our approach specifies a granular replacement depth to frost penetration, installs edge drains to keep the water table below the subbase, and uses field plate load tests to verify subgrade reaction modulus instead of empirical correlations that underestimate clay sensitivity.
