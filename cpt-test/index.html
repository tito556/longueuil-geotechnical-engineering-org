Cone Penetration Testing (CPT) in Longueuil – Continuous Soil Profiling Without Sampling

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Longueuil sits at just 15 meters above sea level, on the thick, sensitive clay deposits of the St. Lawrence Lowlands. This is Champlain Sea sediment. More than 13,000 years old. And it does not forgive assumptions. We run cone penetration tests here because the stratigraphy changes fast – sometimes within 20 horizontal meters you go from stiff silty crust to soft marine clay. A CPT rig pushes a 15 cm² cone at 2 cm/s, and we read tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore pressure every 10 mm. No sampling. No lab delay. The result is a continuous profile of soil behavior type that lets you see exactly where the weak layers sit. For projects near the river or in the Vieux-Longueuil sector, we often pair this with seismic microzonation studies to understand how the clay will respond during a seismic event.

A CPT profile reveals what a borehole log can miss: the thin, high-risk silt seams inside a Champlain clay deposit.

How we work

Winter in Longueuil tests everything. The ground freezes a meter deep some years. That frozen crust over soft clay creates a completely different cone response than the same soil in August. We account for this. Our CPT rigs run a heated cone when needed, and we always measure pore pressure dissipation to separate undrained behavior from partial drainage effects. The CPTu data gives you three measured parameters and one derived: corrected tip resistance (qt), sleeve friction (fs), pore pressure (u2), and friction ratio (Rf). From these we classify soil behavior type using the Robertson chart – no lab sample needed. That said, in zones where the clay shows high sensitivity, we validate the CPT classification with a few Atterberg limits tests to confirm liquidity index and sensitivity. For granular interbeds, which appear frequently near the Saint-Lambert boundary, we cross-check with SPT drilling where the cone refusal indicates dense till or boulders.

Local geotechnical context

In seismic zone C, Longueuil experiences rare high-frequency low-energy tremors from Western Quebec, which can trigger liquefaction in silt lenses. A common oversight in local practice involves assuming the surface crust (brown oxidized silty clay at 2–4 m depth) as the bearing stratum, ignoring underlying materials. Although this crust feels firm with a manual pocket penetrometer, CPT soundings reveal reality: excess pore pressure surges as the cone enters the grey clay below—the zone where embankment failures often initiate. By applying normalized soil behavior type index (Ic) from CPT profiles, contractive silts can be directly identified without waiting for cyclic triaxial lab results. Additionally, abrupt drops in sleeve friction signal sensitive clay prone to retrogression if excavation disturbs it.

Complementary services

01

CPTu profiling with pore pressure dissipation

Our standard procedure involves cone penetration testing with pore pressure measurement at the u2 position. At targeted depths, dissipation tests are executed to derive the coefficient of consolidation for the clay.

02

Seismic CPT (SCPT) add-on

For NBCC seismic site classification, we intermittently acquire downhole shear wave velocity data in conjunction with the cone push, integrating these measurements into the profile.

03

Data interpretation and foundation parameter report

We supply logs of corrected tip resistance (qt), friction ratio (Rf), and soil behavior type index (Ic), along with derived parameters: undrained shear strength (Su) via Nkt factor, constrained modulus (M) for settlement calculations, and equivalent SPT-N60 values.

Relevant standards

The work follows ASTM D5778-20 for electronic friction cone and piezocone testing. Foundation interpretation references CSA A23.3 (Concrete Design). Seismic site class per NBCC 2015 Division B Part 4 is determined using CPT-derived Vs correlations, and classification methodology complies with ISO 22476-1.

Questions and answers

What does a CPT test cost for a typical Longueuil residential lot?

For standard residential investigations in Longueuil, CPT costs typically vary from CA$220 to CA$330 per sounding meter, contingent on depth, site access, and additional dissipation tests. A single-family home lot normally requires two to three soundings.

Can CPT replace boreholes for foundation design in Champlain clay?

Generally, yes—CPT provides a continuous strength profile often superior to SPT data for soft clays. However, for measuring sensitivity or conducting consolidation tests, a few thin-wall Shelby tube samples are still needed. We advocate a hybrid approach: CPT for profiling complemented by one borehole for validation sampling.

How deep can you push a cone in Longueuil's soil?

The refusal depth depends on the till surface. In much of central Longueuil, the Champlain clay extends 20–25 m before encountering dense glacial till. Our rig pushes to refusal when tip resistance exceeds 40 MPa. In the Saint-Hubert area, gravelly outwash may halt the cone at shallower depths.

Do you measure pore pressure during the test?

Yes, our cone is always a piezocone, continuously recording pore water pressure at the u2 position (just behind the tip). This is critical for Longueuil clay, where high excess pore pressure indicates low permeability and undrained behavior, influencing how tip resistance is interpreted for bearing capacity.
