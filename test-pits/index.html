Exploratory Test Pit Services in Longueuil: Reliable Subsurface Data for Your Project

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

Longueuil’s elevation barely grazes 20 meters above the St. Lawrence, and much of the city sits on thick sequences of post-glacial marine clay. When a developer on Chemin de Chambly hit an unexpected pocket of organic silt last spring, the delay cost more than the original survey would have. An exploratory test pit program gives you eyes underground before excavation starts. Our crews open precisely located excavations to log stratigraphy, sample disturbed materials, and measure the depth to the water table. For deeper refusal layers, we often combine the observation with spt-drilling to extend the profile beyond the bucket’s reach. The report you receive is a field document built for foundation designers, not a generic checklist.

A test pit is the only method that lets you walk down and touch the stratigraphy — photos and logs capture what a split spoon never can.

How we work

In Longueuil we consistently encounter a crust of desiccated brown clay over soft grey silty clay — the Champlain Sea deposit. The transition depth varies block by block, sometimes at 1.5 m, sometimes at 4 m. A test pit lets us scrape the wall clean and photograph the contact. We measure moisture content on site with a speedy meter and take bulk samples for lab classification. Because test pits allow visual inspection of fissures and oxidation stains, they often reveal seepage paths that a borehole log would miss. The excavation is backfilled and compacted the same day. For pavement design inputs we can pair the pit with a cbr-road test performed directly on the subgrade surface at the base of the excavation.

Local geotechnical context

The excavator's arm maneuvers into position, its bucket teeth slicing through the asphalt, and within moments the surface layer is breached. In Longueuil, the main danger is the soft clay collapsing at the excavation face if the pit remains open too long, particularly following rainfall. We adhere strictly to CNESST trench safety regulations: any pit deeper than 1.2 m must either have benching at a 1:1 slope or be protected by a trench box. A continuous gas monitor for confined spaces is operated when the excavation goes beyond 1.5 m near old service trenches. Access is arranged through right-of-way permits from the Ville de Longueuil, and a locate request is submitted 72 hours before the machinery arrives. Each pit is backfilled in layers with compaction checks before the crew departs.

Complementary services

01

Standard Exploratory Test Pit

A rubber-tire backhoe reaches the target depth—typically 3 to 4 m—while the field engineer records the exposed profile, obtains disturbed samples, and notes the water table. This approach suits residential and light commercial sites in Vieux-Longueuil and Greenfield Park where access is unobstructed.

02

Pit with In-Situ Testing Package

This includes the same excavation along with sand-cone density testing on the pit floor and a field CBR penetration. Additionally, undisturbed block samples are taken when the clay is sufficiently stiff. This package is intended for road subgrade verification and shallow footing design on the Saint-Hubert plateau.

Relevant standards

We follow ASTM D2488-17e1 (Visual-Manual Description), the CNESST Safety Code for the Construction Industry, CSA A23.3-19 (Concrete Structures—referenced for foundation design input), and NBCC 2020 Division B Part 4.

Questions and answers

How much does an exploratory test pit cost in Longueuil?

For a standard single pit using a rubber-tire backhoe, including a field log, photographs, and a brief report, the cost ranges from CA$610 to CA$1,180. The final price depends on depth, site access, number of pits, and whether additional in-situ tests such as sand cone or CBR are required.

How deep can you go with a test pit in Longueuil's clay?

With a conventional backhoe, we typically achieve depths of 3.5 to 4.0 m in the Champlain clay. Greater depths require a long-reach excavator or borehole drilling. Beyond 4 m, trench stability becomes the primary constraint, and shoring expenses increase significantly.

Do I need a permit for a test pit on my property?

No municipal permit is needed for work on private property, but we always call Info-Excavation for a locate request at least 72 hours in advance. If the pit extends into the city right-of-way—the sidewalk or boulevard—we obtain a temporary obstruction permit from Ville de Longueuil.

Can you take undisturbed samples from a test pit?

We can extract block samples from the pit wall if the clay is sufficiently firm. For very soft sensitive clay, thin-walled Shelby tube sampling using a drill rig is preferred, as block extraction often disturbs the material. We recommend the most suitable method after evaluating the site conditions.

What happens to the pit after you finish logging?

The excavation is filled in on the same day with the original material, placed in 300 mm lifts and compacted using a plate compactor or the backhoe bucket. The surface is left graded and safe. If the client intends to build over the pit location, we recommend including a brief note in the geotechnical report regarding backfill compaction.
