Seismic Tomography (Refraction/Reflection) in Longueuil

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

NBCC 2020, reinforced by local amendments, demands clear subsurface imaging in a city like Longueuil where the contact between overburden and bedrock can shift abruptly over short distances. Seismic refraction and reflection tomography answer that need directly: they map the velocity structure of the ground, flagging weak zones, cavities, and the true depth to refusal long before a backhoe ever hits the site. We run these surveys throughout the South Shore, from Vieux-Longueuil to Saint-Hubert, and the data routinely saves projects from costly guesswork. A seismic refraction line can also be paired with a MASW spread on the same layout to pull Vs30 profiles without doubling the field time.

Reflection tomography in the Champlain Sea clays can pick the bedrock reflector within 0.5 m of the cored depth, even where the bedrock surface dips more than 20 degrees.

How we work

Longueuil sits on the St. Lawrence Lowlands, where glacial till and marine clays of the Champlain Sea blanket a Paleozoic sedimentary bedrock sequence dominated by shale and limestone. Overburden thickness rarely exceeds 30 meters in the central boroughs, but the bedrock surface is frequently irregular, with buried channels and differential weathering that create velocity inversions no borehole log can resolve alone. That is where tomographic processing makes the difference: instead of assuming flat layers, the algorithm iterates thousands of ray paths to reconstruct a 2D velocity cross-section that respects the real geometry. We trigger a sledgehammer or weight-drop source, occasionally a small explosive charge for deeper targets, and the geophone spread captures first arrivals as well as reflected phases when the impedance contrast is strong enough. The result is a continuous image of compressive-wave velocity, directly correlated to rock quality and excavatability. On tighter urban lots, CPT testing provides complementary point data where seismic access is limited by buried utilities.

Local geotechnical context

We frequently encounter situations in Longueuil where contractors base their bids on a few drillholes hitting rock at around 8 meters, only to find a 14-meter-deep channel between holes once excavation begins. The additional expenses from hammering or blasting that rock plus schedule delays quickly deplete the contingency budget. Seismic tomography identifies these channels because it images the entire cross-section rather than single points. Another common issue is mistaking a weathered shale layer for solid bedrock: a seismic velocity of 1,800 m/s can be ripped, whereas 4,000 m/s demands a hydraulic breaker. The tomographic profile provides the contractor with a rippability map for equipment scheduling, and this benefit alone typically covers the survey cost.

Complementary services

01

Seismic Refraction Tomography

For assessing diggability, profiling bedrock, and finding buried valleys, we produce 2D P-wave velocity cross-sections. Our setup uses 24- or 48-channel seismographs with 4.5 Hz geophones, and we process data via time-term inversion and iterative ray-tracing to manage lateral velocity changes common in the St. Lawrence Lowlands.

02

Seismic Reflection Profiling

When targeting deeper structures or encountering a low-velocity layer above a faster one—a situation refraction cannot handle—we perform high-resolution common-midpoint (CMP) surveys. We apply NMO correction, stacking, and post-stack migration to reveal bedrock stratigraphy and fault zones.

03

Combined MASW + Refraction

A single-spread layout delivers both a P-wave velocity model and a Vs30 shear-wave velocity profile. The shear-wave data supports site classification according to NBCC Table 4.1.8.4.A, while the P-wave model addresses excavation ease—two results from one field visit.

Questions and answers

How much does a seismic tomography survey cost in Longueuil?

In Longueuil, a typical refraction line costs between CA$3,930 and CA$7,760, depending on array length, number of shots, and whether reflection processing is included. Paving cuts for geophone placement in urban areas push costs higher. We give a fixed price after reviewing the site plan and target depth.

How deep can refraction tomography see in the Longueuil area?

With a 115-meter array and a weight-drop source, we normally investigate 25 to 30 meters deep—enough to reach competent bedrock across most of the city. If overburden is exceptionally thick or bedrock velocity low, we lengthen the array or use a small explosive source to go deeper.

Can you run a seismic line on a paved parking lot?

Yes, but we drill small holes through asphalt at each geophone location so the spike couples with the soil underneath. We use a rotary hammer for this. Afterward, we patch the holes with cold mix. Field time increases about 15%, but data quality matches that of a grassy site.

What is the difference between refraction and reflection tomography for a foundation job?

Refraction measures the velocity of the first-arriving wave and works best when velocity increases with depth—typical in Longueuil, where soft clay overlies limestone. Reflection directly images impedance contrasts, so it detects velocity reversals like a loose sand lens under stiff clay that refraction would miss. For most foundation studies, we begin with refraction and add reflection if the geology requires it.
